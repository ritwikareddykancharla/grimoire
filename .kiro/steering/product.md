# Product: Grimoire '95

Grimoire '95 resurrects the 1990s multimedia CD-ROM encyclopedia (think Encarta, Grolier, Compton's) as a web app powered by live Wikipedia data. It is our entry in the **Kiroween 2026 Resurrection** category.

## Who it is for
- Curious kids (8–14) and the parents and teachers who want a calm, ad-free place for them to explore knowledge.
- Adults who miss the feeling of wandering an encyclopedia instead of doom-scrolling search results.

## The problem
Wikipedia is the best free knowledge source ever built, but its pages are dense, citation-heavy and designed for editors as much as readers. 90s encyclopedias made exploring feel like play: media galleries, timelines, and a quiz game (MindMaze) that rewarded curiosity. That experience died with the CD-ROM.

## Product pillars
1. **Exploration over search.** Every screen should invite a next click: related links, "on this day", random articles, the game.
2. **Readable for kids.** The Simple/Full reading level toggle switches between Simple English Wikipedia and full English Wikipedia.
3. **Play teaches.** Mind Manor (our haunted MindMaze) builds riddles from real Wikipedia history and always links to the article behind the answer.
4. **Zero friction.** No accounts, no API keys, no backend. It opens and works.
5. **Honest attribution.** Wikipedia content is CC BY-SA 4.0. Every article view credits and links to the original.

## Tone and look
- Windows-95-era chrome (bevels, title bars, status bar) with a Halloween palette: plum, violet, pumpkin, bone-white parchment.
- Spooky but friendly. Ghosts, lanterns and fog are playful, never gory. Copy is short and a little theatrical ("Summoning…", "The door creaks open.").
- Article text stays highly readable: serif body, generous line height, parchment background.

## Non-goals
- Editing Wikipedia, user accounts, or saving data on a server.
- Using third-party trademarks (we say "inspired by 90s CD-ROM encyclopedias", never brand the app as one).
